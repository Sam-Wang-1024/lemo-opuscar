## Claims

| Claim | Source | Wording |
|---|---|---|
| The Sun printed "The Clock That Went Backward" on 18 Sept 1881 | the page itself (masthead "THE SUN, SUNDAY, SEPTEMBER 18, 1881", page 2, headline top left) | "THE CLOCK THAT WENT BACKWARD." |
| It ran unsigned | the page itself (no byline under the headline); Wikipedia, *The Clock that Went Backward* | "published anonymously in the New York newspaper The Sun on September 18, 1881" |
| The clock's hands move backward | the page, col. 1, OCR line coords (262,5479)–(583,5537) | "The hands of the clock were moving; they were moving backward." |
| The professor's question | the page, OCR line coords (974,5975)–(1664,6002) and neighbours | "Well, and why should not a clock go backward? Why should not Time itself turn and retrace its course?" |
| The story takes its characters back to the siege of Leyden (1574) | the page (OCR: "a huge breach was made in the wall of Leyden"; "remaining that antedate 1574") | — |
| Regarded as the first time travel by a mechanical device | Wikipedia, *The Clock that Went Backward* | "has been regarded as the first known instance of using a mechanical device for time travel" — said in the film only as "a clock", not "the first" |
| The author was Edward Page Mitchell; his stories were collected in 1973 | Wikipedia (same page); SF Encyclopedia, "Mitchell, Edward Page" | "Sam Moskowitz rediscovered Mitchell's stories and collected them in The Crystal Man (1973)" |
| *El Anacronópete* published Barcelona 1887 | the title page | "BARCELONA … DANIEL CORTEZO y C.ª … 1887" |
| "anacronópete" = one who flies against time | Wikipedia, *El anacronópete* | "a neologism for 'who flies against time'" |
| One of the first stories with a machine that travels through time | Wikipedia, *El anacronópete* | "one of the first to feature a machine that travels through time" |
| The ship rises like a balloon | page 63 | "El Anacronópete se elevaba con la majestad de un montgolfier." (tr.: "The Anacronópete rose with the majesty of a Montgolfier balloon.") |

| Heinemann edition published 29 May 1895 | Wikipedia, *The Time Machine* | "Heinemann published a British edition on 29 May." |
| Wells coined the term "time machine" | Wikipedia, *The Time Machine* | "The term 'time machine', coined by Wells, is now almost universally used" |
